This bibliography maps recurring external sources cited in the 2021–2022 archive. Each entry is a source used in at least two posts in a given year; counts show link occurrences and distinct posts, not unique articles, readership or influence. The annotations describe how sources were used, not their reliability or endorsement. It follows the format of the 2023–2026 bibliography.

Research and analysis

Bellingcat4 links · 4 posts · 2021-01-25–2021-11-25

Open-source investigations group. The archive follows its announced investigation into the failed 2020 operation to lure Wagner PMC fighters from Minsk. The January 25 post notes the report had been promised but not yet published. The March 14 post cites a 2016 Bellingcat report on Russian artillery strikes into Ukraine as the source of a map. The November 18 and 25 posts cite its November 17 report, titled “Inside Wagnergate: Ukraine’s Brazen Sting Operation to Snare Russian Mercenaries,” which the November 25 post describes as an 11,000-word investigation. The posts report Bellingcat's findings next to the author's own argument about who ended the operation.

Representative links: report (2021-11-18); report (2021-03-14).

Method and files

Dates attached to individual source citations are the dates of the citing posts as they appear in the archive files, not the publication dates of the cited items. Date ranges in the entry headings describe when a publisher was cited in the archive, not necessarily the publisher’s publication dates. Post titles in 2022 (“Day N, Phase M”) do not run on a continuous war-day count, so the annotations cite calendar dates.

This bibliography lists recurring external sources cited in the archive: a publisher or source is included for a given year when it appears in two or more posts during that year. Links back to the author’s own archive and sites are excluded. The same publisher may appear in both years because the counts and annotations describe that year’s use.

The two annual link files contain 1,447 link occurrences, of which 271 point to the author’s own archive and sites and are excluded. 237 posts have at least one link (40 posts in 2021 and 203 in 2022 in all). Excluding the own-archive links, the links identify 277 source domains and 251 normalized publishers, and the files hold 1,149 distinct URL strings. Counts are link occurrences and distinct posts, not unique articles, readership or influence. Publisher aliases are consolidated using the 2023–2026 source registry, extended with the additions listed in the domain-alias file. Representative links are examples, not an exhaustive list of every item cited.

Annotations identify the source category and describe how the archive uses it. They do not rate reliability or imply endorsement. Source categories are drawn from the normalized registry. Social media, business, specialist and unclassified publishers are grouped under “Platforms, services and specialist references”. C-SPAN is placed under reference and archives, as in the 2023–2026 audit.

Facebook and Google redirect links were unwrapped to their destinations before counting, so the cited publisher is credited and not the redirect service. Bitly short links could not be resolved from the working environment, so they are listed as their own entry and are not credited to the publishers they may lead to. The 2021 file contains two versions of the November post “Bring Back Our Blimp” (dated November 12 and November 14), and both are counted as separate posts.

This 2021–2022 bibliography is a separate page that can be merged with the 2023–2026 bibliography. It uses the same styles, the same category order and the same year and category anchors. Source files: 2021.md and 2022.md. Data files: working_set.
