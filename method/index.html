Method

How the record is assembled, what its figures mean, and how corrections are handled.

Scope reviewed November 5, 2026. The infrastructure chronology is a live record. Dashboard measures labelled “since October 1” use October 1 as their start date. The fatality minimum is through November 5. Kyiv alert figures are through the 16:38 all-clear on November 5. Other measures retain their individual source dates.

Working in a live war zone

We are reporting from Kyiv during an active war, under conditions that are uncomfortable, dangerous and likely to become more difficult and confusing. We do our best to be fair and accurate with the information available to us, but this is a working record, not a complete census of every attack, damaged site or fatality.

Prosecutors, police, city and regional administrations, emergency services and other official bodies may publish different figures or update them at different times. After a major strike, casualty counts often rise as rescue operations continue and additional victims are found. Earlier figures are not necessarily final; later revisions can also correct an initial report. We attribute figures to their sources, retain the distinction between a source's statement and our own observation, and revise the record when better information becomes available.

Uncertainty is not a reason to hide a reported death or to pretend the count is complete. Nor is it a licence to invent a margin. We publish the best-supported working figure we can assemble, explain its limits and correct it as the record changes.

Fatalities

The dashboard displays a provisional nationwide documented minimum of approximately 236 fatalities reported in connection with Russian attacks since October 1. It is not an official national total and does not cover every fatality in Ukraine. It counts all reported fatalities unless a person is specifically identified as military personnel. Police officers, rescuers and other emergency personnel are included. Injuries are excluded. People listed as missing are not counted unless their deaths are confirmed.

We compare reports from Ukrainian authorities and Ukrainian and international media. We do not add broad regional totals to incident-level figures where doing so could count the same deaths twice. Where sources disagree, we attribute the figures and reconcile them where possible rather than silently treating different reports as separate deaths. We do not add an unquantified estimate for deaths that may be missing from published reporting.

The current minimum includes 43 people reported killed in the November 2 strike on Komyshuvakha, two police officers who died in hospital after a separate November 1 drone strike nearby, and three people killed in Zaporizhia district on November 4. A further audit added six deaths from October 30 attacks or the October 29–30 Dnipropetrovsk reporting window: two in Sloviansk, one near Shostka, one in Nikopol district, one in Dnipro district and one in Orikhiv. These six had not been included in the earlier subtotal. The two Dnipropetrovsk district deaths occurred after the October 29 Dnipro citywide month-to-date subtotal and are separate from it.

Dnipro Mayor Borys Filatov reported nine other Dnipro residents killed in Russian attacks during October, in addition to the 14 killed in the October 29 railway-station strike. The nine are a citywide month-to-date subtotal, not nine new deaths on October 29. They are not added separately to the incident-level deaths already recorded.

DTEK confirmed that driver Vitaliy Shcherbyna and miner Valentyn Kotelevskyi were killed in separate attacks on October 25 and 26. Kotelevskyi corresponds to the Pavlohrad death reported by the regional administration on October 26. The public reports do not establish whether Shcherbyna was already included in the October 25 Dnipropetrovsk regional casualty total carried into the baseline. He is not added separately pending source-level deduplication, so the displayed minimum may be understated by one.

Reconciliation audit

We conduct an incident-by-incident audit of the fatality minimum against the chronology and daily casualty roundups, including Ukraine24h's visual maps. This checks both the written record and the arithmetic. Reports that cannot yet be matched to a specific incident remain unresolved, not automatically added. Some may already be included in an earlier subtotal, and later roundups can repeat deaths reported in previous editions.

Daily roundups do not always describe deaths occurring on that calendar date. Some cover rolling 24-hour windows, and some report a death days after the original attack, including when an injured person dies in hospital. We assign deaths to the incident date where it can be established, compare the underlying places and incidents, and check for overlap before changing the subtotal. We preserve the distinction between a documented death, an unresolved report and a possible duplicate.

The audit is ongoing. The published figure remains a provisional documented minimum, not a complete national count. We take each reported fatality seriously, including the possibility that our own record is incomplete, and will correct it as the comparison establishes what should be added or revised.

Kyiv air alerts

The dashboard currently records 251 provisional Kyiv alerts and approximately 219 hours 30 minutes under alert through the 16:38 all-clear on November 5. The figures are provisional working totals from October 1, assembled from compiler-supplied intervals and cross-checked against the official Kyiv Digital interval history and alerts.in.ua. They are not a reconstructed official total. Start and end times may differ between trackers or be rounded in a supplied snapshot. Tracker-reported durations are retained where they differ from displayed clock endpoints. Unresolved intervals are excluded rather than assigned guessed durations.

Earlier snapshots and unresolved interval differences are retained in the chronology update history. The October 22 record, for example, preserves a discrepancy between an 11:22 compiler-supplied start and an 11:24 published daily-list start. The working count uses one interval, not two. Older intervals without sufficient evidence remain excluded.

The reference sources are alerts.in.ua and Kyiv Digital official alert history. They may lag or disagree, and alert boundaries may be rounded in supplied snapshots. A general Kyiv air alert does not prove that a ballistic threat was identified with useful lead time.

Facilities and bridge strikes

The working site list covers physical facilities or separately identified sites reported struck, damaged or credibly affected since October 1. It includes residential and commercial premises as well as infrastructure. An entry is not necessarily one physical structure: one entry may group multiple premises or facilities, while a broad-area debris report may be excluded if a distinct location cannot be established. Repeated strikes on the same facility do not automatically create new entries. The current dashboard shows 59 entries in the working list through October 29, not 59 verified physical sites.

The bridge figure counts reported hits on Kyiv bridges since October 1, not a structural-damage assessment. The working count is at least nine: at least seven reported hits involving the South Bridge and two involving the North Bridge. A later smoke plume was not counted as an additional hit because the chronology did not have a linked, independently verifiable source establishing a separate impact.

Evidence labels

Labels describe the source or evidentiary basis of an entry. They are not a numerical confidence score, and a label does not by itself mean that every detail has been independently confirmed.

  • Official — a statement or report from a public authority, including police, prosecutors, emergency services, the Air Force, or city and regional administrations.
  • Operator or company — information published by an infrastructure operator, utility, transport provider or affected business.
  • Media — reporting by a news organisation. The source note should identify the report; media coverage is not automatically independent corroboration of an official statement.
  • Eyewitness — the author's direct observation, with time and place given where relevant. It establishes what was observed, not necessarily the weapon, cause or full extent of damage.
  • Attributed — a claim assigned to a named person or institution that has not been independently established in the entry.
  • Tracker — a timestamp or status recorded by an alert or event-tracking service; tracker times may lag, be rounded or share underlying data.
  • Unverified — a claim for which adequate corroboration has not been found. It is not treated as established fact, and is excluded from quantitative totals where appropriate.
  • Editorial — a calculation, synthesis or interpretation made by the compiler rather than a direct quotation from a source.

Several evidence types may inform an entry. Source links are supplied where available. If an original post disappears or a source changes its report, that limitation may affect how confidently the entry can be checked later.

Sources and attribution

We prefer direct statements from relevant Ukrainian authorities, emergency services, operators and companies, and use Ukrainian and international media to corroborate or contextualise them. We try to link to the specific source item rather than a general channel feed. A source's figure is attributed to that source; it is not presented as our independent finding unless we have independently established it.

Reports can be deleted, edited or moved. Where practical, important source records may be preserved, but not every link can be archived and a live link is not a guarantee that the source remains unchanged. Absence of a surviving link does not itself prove a claim false; it does mean that the claim is harder to verify.

Corrections and updates

This is a working chronology updated when credible new reporting becomes available and time permits. It is not staffed continuously, and an update may lag events, particularly during an attack. We do not promise a fixed daily publication time.

When a source revises a casualty figure, location, damage report or attribution, we update the relevant entry and the dashboard when the change affects a displayed measure. Material changes are described in the chronology's public update history. We aim to preserve the substance of earlier reporting where useful to show how a figure changed, rather than silently erasing a material correction. Technical maintenance is not a factual correction and should be kept separate from the public corrections record.

Corrections are made when credible new evidence warrants them. If a reader identifies an error or has a relevant primary source, please contact the author through the ukraine@war Substack publication. Include the chronology entry or date, the claim in question, and the source supporting the correction.

Safety and limits

We do not publish information simply because it is available. During an ongoing attack, precise impact locations, imagery of air-defence activity and other details may create risks; publication decisions must account for timing, source reliability and operational sensitivity. A published location is approximate unless a source establishes more. Images may also reveal identifying details that are not obvious from their captions.

This record is assembled under wartime conditions. It is a best-effort public account, open to correction, and should not be read as a complete national casualty tally or an exhaustive inventory of damage.
